On May 6, 2026, I defended my master's thesis in the Graduate Program in Computer Science at the Federal University of Goiás (PPGCC/UFG), and **it was approved**.

The dissertation, **Industrial Scaling Laws for Continual Pre-Training: Optimizing Intra-Domain Mixtures**, was supervised by Professor Arlindo Rodrigues Galvão Filho.

## What I studied

The research asks how data selection shapes the adaptation of language models to industrial settings. In these domains, technical manuals and standards sit alongside maintenance reports: sources that belong to the same domain but differ in structure and content.

I modeled how model scale, token exposure and the proportions of these sources interact during continual pre-training. The goal is practical: to guide how a training budget should be split across the different needs within a domain.

## The defense

The defense was a chance to explain my research choices, discuss the limitations and think through next steps. Approval closes this stage and gives me a starting point for future work.

I thank my advisor, **Professor Arlindo Rodrigues Galvão Filho**, for his guidance throughout the master's and for the discussions that shaped this work, and the examining committee, **Professor Telma Woerle de Lima Soares** and **Professor Rodrigo Zempulski Fanucchi**, for reading the dissertation and for their questions and insights during the defense.

A dissertation brings together experiments and writing, but it also reflects the people who make that work possible. I am grateful to everyone who supported me along the way.
